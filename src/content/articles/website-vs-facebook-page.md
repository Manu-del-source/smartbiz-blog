"I already have a Facebook Page, why do I need a website too?" is one of the most common questions small business owners ask, and it's a fair one — a Facebook Page is free, quick to set up, and reaches people where they already spend time. The honest answer is that a Page and a website aren't really doing the same job, and treating one as a substitute for the other leaves real gaps.

## What a Facebook Page Does Well

Facebook Pages (and WhatsApp Business alongside them) are genuinely good at reaching an existing audience quickly, sharing updates and offers, and letting customers message you directly with minimal friction. For a lot of day-to-day customer interaction, that's exactly what's needed.

## Where a Facebook Page Falls Short

- **You don't own it.** Meta can restrict, suspend, or limit a Page's reach at any time, for reasons entirely outside your control — and it does happen, sometimes for reasons owners never fully understand.
- **It's not built to be found by strangers searching Google.** Someone searching "[your service] in [your area]" is far more likely to land on a website than a Facebook Page, since Google generally doesn't index Facebook content the same way.
- **Your posts compete with everyone else's.** Organic reach on Facebook has declined significantly over the years — a Page with thousands of followers can still see only a small fraction of them actually see any given post without paid boosting.
- **It can look less credible for bigger decisions.** For higher-value purchases, B2B customers, or anyone doing due diligence before committing, a real website still tends to read as more established.

## Where a Website Wins

A website is something you fully own and control, is built to be discovered by people who've never heard of your business before, and works as a permanent, always-available reference point — your price list, your portfolio, your contact details — without needing an active social media presence to keep functioning.

## The Realistic Answer: Use Both, Differently

This isn't really an either-or decision. The strongest setup for most Kenyan small businesses is a website as the credible, owned home base — the place a serious customer ends up before deciding to buy — with a Facebook Page and WhatsApp Business handling the daily conversation and driving traffic toward that home base.
