"I already have a Facebook Page — why do I need a website too?" It's one of the most common questions small business owners ask, and it's a fair one. A Page is free, takes an afternoon to set up, and reaches people where they already spend time. The honest answer is that a Page and a website aren't doing the same job, and treating one as a substitute for the other leaves real gaps. Here's how to think about each.

## What a Facebook Page does well

Facebook Pages (with WhatsApp Business alongside them) are genuinely good at:

- **Reaching your existing audience quickly** — new stock, today's offers, changed hours, event announcements.
- **Two-way conversation** — customers message you directly with minimal friction, in an app they already use daily.
- **Social proof in context** — comments, shares, and tagged photos from real customers, visible to their friends.
- **Low-effort selling** — posting items with prices and taking orders in the comments or DMs works well for many small retailers.

For day-to-day customer interaction, that combination is hard to beat, and no website replaces it. If your business runs on repeat customers and word of mouth, your Page and WhatsApp number are doing real commercial work. Keep them.

## Where a Facebook Page falls short

- **You don't own it.** Meta can restrict a Page's reach, suspend it, or change what your followers see, for reasons entirely outside your control — algorithm shifts, mistaken automated flags, policy changes. Businesses do lose their primary customer channel overnight this way. It is rare, but the risk is structural: you are a tenant, not an owner.
- **It's not built to be found by strangers.** Someone searching Google for "[your service] in [your area]" is far more likely to land on a website than a Facebook Page. Google indexes websites deeply and Facebook content only shallowly, so discovery-by-search barely works through a Page.
- **Your posts compete with everything else.** Organic reach on Facebook has declined substantially over the years — a Page with thousands of followers can easily see only a small fraction of them shown any given post unless you pay to boost it. The audience you "built" is one you increasingly have to rent back.
- **It can look thin for bigger decisions.** For higher-value purchases, corporate clients, or anyone doing due diligence before committing, a real website still reads as more established. A Page alone invites the question "is this a serious business?" at exactly the moment you need the opposite answer.
- **Information is hard to organize.** Menus, price lists, service details, and portfolios get buried in the timeline within days. Pinning helps; it doesn't fix the underlying format.

## Where a website wins

A website is the owned home base: something you fully control, built to be discovered by people who've never heard of you, working as a permanent reference — price list, portfolio, booking page, contact details — without needing daily posting to keep functioning. It shows up in Google searches, it can't be suspended by a platform, and it converts the interested visitor your social channels sent over. (The fuller case is in [why every small business in Kenya needs a website](/why-small-business-kenya-needs-website).)

The trade-off is real too: a website costs money to build and requires some maintenance, while a Page is free. That's why the decision should be sequenced, not agonized over — see below.

## Side-by-side comparison

| | Facebook Page + WhatsApp | Website |
|---|---|---|
| Setup cost | Free | Tens of thousands of KES ([typical ranges](/how-much-does-website-cost-kenya)) |
| Found by strangers on Google | Weak | Strong (if built for it) |
| Daily customer conversation | Excellent | Poor (contact forms can't compete with chat) |

| Organized info (menu, prices) | Buried in timeline | Permanent, structured pages |
| Credibility for big-ticket/B2B | Thin | Strong |
| Ongoing effort | Daily posting to stay visible | Occasional updates once built |

## The realistic answer: sequence them

This isn't an either-or decision — it's a sequence. The setup that works for most Kenyan small businesses:

1. **Start with what you have.** If the Page and WhatsApp are bringing orders, keep running them. Add a free [Google Business Profile](https://business.google.com/) so searches for your business name show correct details.
2. **Secure your domain early.** Even before you build a site, registering your `.co.ke` or `.com` name through an accredited registrar (see [KeNIC](https://kenic.or.ke/)) is cheap insurance against losing it. Our [domain and hosting guide](/domain-hosting-guide-kenya) explains how.
3. **Add the website when the business justifies it** — typically when you're losing customers who search first, pitching higher-value work, or tired of re-sending your price list in DMs. Make sure it includes [the elements that actually matter](/what-good-business-website-includes) rather than just existing.
4. **Run both, with distinct jobs:** the website as the credible home base where serious customers land and decide; the Page and WhatsApp as the daily conversation layer driving traffic toward it.

Businesses that do this stop choosing between channels and start compounding them — every social follower who bookmarks the site, and every search visitor who follows the Page, becomes reachable twice.
