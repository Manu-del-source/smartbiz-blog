Your Google Business Profile is the free listing that puts your business on Google Search and Maps — with your hours, phone number, location, photos, and reviews, often displayed above every website result. For a local business, it's the highest-leverage free marketing asset available. Yet most profiles we see are half-finished: wrong hours, no photos, three reviews from two years ago. This guide walks through doing it properly, start to finish.

If you haven't read the bigger picture yet, start with [how to get found on Google](/how-to-get-found-on-google-kenya) — this guide is the detailed walkthrough for step one of that checklist.

## Before you start: what you'll need

- A Google account for the business (not a personal one you'll lose access to — use an address the business controls permanently).
- Your exact business name, as it appears on your signage.
- Your physical address (or the service area you cover, if you visit customers).
- Your phone number, website address, and opening hours.
- A few genuine photos ready: exterior, interior, team or work in progress.

## Step 1: Create or claim your profile

Go to [business.google.com](https://business.google.com/) and sign in. Enter your business name:

- **If Google suggests your business already exists** (common — Google auto-generates listings from various sources), claim it rather than creating a duplicate. You'll need to verify ownership, covered below.
- **If it doesn't exist**, create it. Enter the name exactly as customers know it — don't add keywords or slogans to the name field ("Best Salon Nairobi Cheap" violates Google's guidelines and risks suspension).

Choose your business type carefully: storefront (customers visit you), service-area business (you visit them, e.g. plumbing, deliveries), or hybrid. This choice affects what Google shows — service-area businesses, for example, can hide their home address while still showing the areas they cover.

## Step 2: Verify ownership

Google needs proof the business is yours before your profile goes fully live. It will offer one or more verification options — commonly a video recording of your premises, a postcard mailed to your address with a code, or in some cases instant verification. The options shown vary by business type and location.

Practical notes from experience:

- **Follow whichever option Google shows you.** Don't wait for a "better" method — verification options don't improve with patience.
- **For video verification**, film in one continuous take: your signage, the street outside, inside the premises, proof you operate there (stock, equipment, branded materials). Blurry or edited footage gets rejected.
- **For postcards**, allow up to a couple of weeks for delivery in Kenya, and don't edit core details (name, address, category) while waiting — changes can restart the process.
- **Don't create a second profile** if verification is slow. Duplicates cause far worse problems than delays.

## Step 3: Complete every section

Verification gets you listed; completeness gets you *chosen*. Work through all of these:

- **Categories.** Your primary category is the most important choice on the profile — it's how Google decides which searches you qualify for. Pick the closest match to what you actually do, then add secondary categories if they genuinely apply.
- **Hours — including special hours.** Regular hours, plus public holidays and any seasonal changes. Wrong hours that send a customer to a closed shop cost more than a ranking ever gains.
- **Description.** A genuine paragraph about what you do, in normal language. Mention your area naturally.
- **Services and products.** List what you offer with enough detail that a customer can pre-qualify themselves.
- **Attributes.** Wheelchair access, women-led, payment options accepted (including M-Pesa where relevant) — these answer real customer questions before they're asked.
- **Photos.** Exterior (so customers recognize you from the street), interior, team, products or finished work. Then keep adding: a few new phone photos every month or two signals an active business far better than a polished-but-ancient gallery.
- **Website and appointment/ordering links.** Connect your site, and add direct links for booking, ordering, or WhatsApp where the profile supports them.

## Step 4: Set up reviews properly

Reviews are both a ranking signal and the deciding factor for customers comparing listings. Before you ask anyone:

- **Generate your direct review link** from your Business Profile dashboard — a short link that takes customers straight to the review form. Long instructions ("search for us, scroll down, click reviews...") kill response rates; one tap works.
- **Decide when you'll ask.** The right moment is right after a good experience: a completed job, a finished meal, a successful delivery. Build it into the routine, not into a someday campaign.

The full playbook — what to say, how to respond, and how to handle unfair reviews — is in [how to get online reviews](/get-online-reviews-kenya-business).

## Step 5: Use posts and updates

Profiles support short posts: offers, events, new products, announcements. You don't need a content strategy — a post when something genuinely newsworthy happens (new stock, holiday hours, a completed project with photos) keeps the profile visibly alive. Think of it as a noticeboard, not a blog.

## Step 6: Check your performance monthly

Your profile dashboard shows how customers find you (direct name searches vs. discovery category searches), and what they do (calls, direction requests, website visits). Thirty minutes a month on these numbers tells you more than any ranking report:

- **Discovery searches growing?** Your visibility for category terms is improving.
- **Calls and directions growing?** Visibility is converting into business.
- **Stuck flat?** Usually a completeness problem (missing info, few photos) or a reviews problem — both fixable.

## Mistakes that get profiles suspended or suppressed

- Keyword-stuffed business names.
- Fake, bought, or incentivized reviews — Google removes these in waves and penalizes patterns.
- Wrong address or service area (including using a virtual address where you don't operate).
- Letting a former employee or agency own the profile — ownership must sit with the business permanently. If someone else set yours up, get primary ownership transferred to a business-controlled account now, before you need it.

## What to do today

If you have no profile: create it today — the process starts with ten minutes of form-filling, and verification runs in the background. If you have one: audit it against step 3 this week, generate your review link, and ask your next five satisfied customers. The businesses that dominate local search aren't doing anything exotic. They just finished the form.
