Food delivery apps have become a default way many Kenyans discover and order from restaurants — Glovo, Uber Eats, and Bolt Food between them handle a large share of online food ordering in the country. That reach is genuinely useful, especially for reaching customers who weren't looking for you by name. It also comes with structural costs: delivery platforms take a commission on every order, control how your menu and photos are presented, and own the customer relationship. A diner who orders through an app is the app's customer first — next time they're hungry, they open the app, not your number.

A simple restaurant website closes that gap without requiring you to abandon the delivery apps that already work for you.

## The commission maths, illustrated

Delivery commissions take a double-digit percentage of every order — the exact figure depends on your agreement, so use your own number here. For illustration: on a KES 1,000 order at a 25% commission, KES 250 goes to the platform. A restaurant doing 200 delivery orders a month at that average gives up roughly KES 50,000 monthly. Shift a fifth of those regulars to ordering direct — via your site or WhatsApp — and that's ~KES 10,000 a month kept, permanently, for the cost of a simple website and the habit of asking.

The point isn't that delivery apps are bad value. For *discovery* — a first-time customer who found you while browsing — the commission is a fair customer-acquisition cost. The waste is paying it on the *tenth* order from a regular who would happily order direct if you made it easy. Your website exists to convert the second kind of order.

## What a delivery app listing doesn't give you

- **Google visibility.** A listing inside an app doesn't rank when someone searches "restaurant in [your area]" or "[cuisine] near me" — a website with a complete [Google Business Profile](/google-business-profile-setup-guide-kenya) does, and those searches are exactly how new customers pick somewhere to eat.
- **A direct relationship.** You can't message app customers about a new dish, a weekend offer, or changed hours. Your own channel — site plus WhatsApp — can.
- **Control of presentation.** On the app you're one tile among dozens of competitors, photographed and described to the platform's template. On your site, your best dishes lead.
- **Customer data.** Repeat-order patterns, popular dishes, slow nights — the information you'd use to run the business better stays with the platform.

## What a restaurant website actually needs

Restaurants don't need complicated websites. They need fast, simple ones that answer the questions a hungry customer actually has:

- **An up-to-date menu with prices**, viewable instantly — no PDF downloads, no account creation, no "call for prices." Stale prices are worse than no prices; update the site the day the menu changes.
- **A WhatsApp ordering or reservation button**, prominent on every page. For many Kenyan diners, tapping through to WhatsApp is lower-friction than any web form — and the conversation it starts is a direct customer relationship.
- **Real photos of your actual food.** Not stock imagery, not photos from another restaurant — this is the single biggest trust signal for a restaurant specifically. Phone photos of real plates beat professional shots of someone else's food.
- **Location, hours, and a map link** that's accurate and easy to find. First-time visitors deciding between you and the place next door will pick the one they can actually locate.
- **Genuine customer reviews**, whether pulled from Google or displayed directly. Diners check social proof before committing to somewhere new — see [how to build your reviews](/get-online-reviews-kenya-business).
- **Your story in two paragraphs.** Who runs the kitchen, what the place is known for, since when. It costs nothing and it's what turns "a listing" into "our place."

## Use both, strategically

The realistic approach isn't choosing between delivery apps and a website — it's assigning each the job it's good at:

| Channel | Job |
|---|---|
| Delivery apps | Discovery and first orders from people browsing by craving, not by name |
| Your website + Google presence | Converting searchers ("near me" traffic) and giving regulars a direct path |
| WhatsApp | Repeat orders, reservations, and the ongoing relationship |

Make the handoff explicit. Put a small card in every delivery bag: your WhatsApp number, your site, and one reason to order direct next time (faster, a free extra, loyalty tracking). Mention it on the phone when regulars call. The apps will keep delivering discovery; your job is making sure discovery converts into *your* customer, not a permanent commission stream.

Start with the menu page, the photos, the WhatsApp button, and the Business Profile — that combination alone captures most of the value. Everything else can follow once direct orders start coming in.
