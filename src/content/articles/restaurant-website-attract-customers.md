Food delivery apps have become the default way many Kenyans discover and order from restaurants — Glovo, Uber Eats, and Bolt Food between them handle a large share of online food ordering in the country, with Kenya's Competition Authority reporting Glovo alone holding roughly a third of consumer preference in the category, ahead of Uber Eats and Bolt Food. That reach is genuinely useful. It also comes at a cost: delivery platforms take a commission on every order, control how your menu and photos are presented, and own the customer relationship — a diner who orders through an app is the app's customer first, not necessarily yours.

## What a Delivery App Listing Doesn't Give You

A listing on a delivery platform doesn't let you rank in Google search results for "restaurant in [your area]," doesn't let you build a direct relationship with regulars, and doesn't give you full control over your presentation — you're one tile among dozens of competitors on someone else's app. A simple restaurant website closes that gap without requiring you to abandon the delivery apps that already work for you.

## What a Restaurant Website Actually Needs

Restaurants don't need a complicated website — they need a fast, simple one that answers the questions a hungry customer actually has:

- **An up-to-date menu with prices**, viewable without downloading anything or creating an account.
- **A WhatsApp ordering or reservation button** — for many Kenyan diners, tapping through to WhatsApp is a lower-friction path to an order than any web form.
- **Real photos of your actual food**, not generic stock imagery — this is one of the biggest trust signals for a restaurant specifically.
- **Location, hours, and a map link** that's accurate and easy to find, especially for first-time visitors trying to locate you.
- **A Google Business Profile linked to the site**, since "restaurant near me" and "[cuisine] in [area]" searches are exactly how a lot of new customers find a place to eat.
- **Genuine customer reviews**, whether pulled from Google or displayed directly — diners look for social proof before committing to a new place.

## Use Both, Strategically

The realistic approach for most Kenyan restaurants isn't choosing between delivery apps and a website — it's using each for what it's good at. Delivery apps are strong for discovery and one-off orders from people who weren't already looking for you by name. A website is what turns a first-time customer into someone who orders directly next time, without the commission and without competing for attention against every other restaurant listed on the same app.
