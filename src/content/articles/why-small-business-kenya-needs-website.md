Kenya's mobile and mobile money infrastructure is genuinely strong — the Communications Authority of Kenya's latest figures put active mobile subscriptions above 88 million with mobile money subscriptions above 54 million, up double digits year-on-year. Most small business owners already know this and have responded by building a presence on WhatsApp Business or a Facebook Page. That's a reasonable first step. It's also, on its own, an incomplete one.

## What a Facebook Page or WhatsApp Business Can't Do

A social media page is genuinely useful for reaching people who already follow you, but it has real limits: you don't control it (an account can be restricted, suspended, or have its reach quietly reduced by an algorithm change), it's not built to be found by someone who's never heard of your business and is searching Google for what you sell, and it puts you in the same crowded feed as every other business competing for attention — including your direct competitors' ads.

## What Owning a Website Actually Changes

- **You show up in search, not just in feeds.** A customer searching "[your service] in [your town]" on Google can find a website. They generally can't find a Facebook Page the same way.
- **You control it.** No platform can suspend your account, change the algorithm on you, or bury your page behind a "pay to boost this post" wall.
- **It signals legitimacy.** Fairly or not, a real website still reads as more established and trustworthy to a new customer than a social page alone — particularly for higher-value purchases or B2B customers doing due diligence.
- **It works while you're not online.** A website doesn't require you to be actively posting to keep functioning as your storefront, price list, or booking page.

## What It Actually Costs to Get Started

This is usually where the idea stalls — many small business owners assume a "real" website is a large, out-of-reach expense. In practice, a simple landing page for a small Kenyan business typically starts in the tens of thousands of shillings, not hundreds of thousands, with more complex sites (e-commerce, custom booking systems, M-Pesa integration) costing more depending on what they need to do. ## You Don't Have to Choose

None of this means abandoning WhatsApp or Facebook — for many small businesses, social media is still where a lot of day-to-day customer conversation happens, and that's fine. The strongest setup for most small businesses is a website as the credible, owned home base, with social media and WhatsApp feeding traffic into it.
