Kenya's mobile and mobile-money infrastructure is genuinely strong — the Communications Authority of Kenya's quarterly sector statistics reports (published on [ca.go.ke](https://www.ca.go.ke/)) have consistently put active mobile subscriptions in the tens of millions, with tens of millions of mobile money subscriptions alongside them. Most small business owners have responded sensibly: build a presence on WhatsApp Business or a Facebook Page, where the customers already are. That's a reasonable first step. On its own, it's also an incomplete one. Here's what changes when you add a website you own.

## You show up in search, not just in feeds

This is the single biggest gap. A customer searching Google for "[your service] in [your town]" — a plumber in Kapsabet, a salon in Kisumu, a supplier of dairy equipment in Eldoret — can find a website. They generally cannot find a Facebook Page the same way, because Google indexes websites deeply and social content only shallowly.

Think about your own behavior: when you need something from a business you've never used before, you search. Your customers do exactly the same, including the ones who will never see your Facebook posts because the algorithm never shows them. A website puts you in that discovery path; without one, you're invisible to every customer whose journey starts with a search. Our [local SEO checklist](/how-to-get-found-on-google-kenya) covers how to win those searches once the site exists.

## You control it

No platform can suspend your website, throttle its reach, or bury it behind a "pay to boost this post" wall. Your domain is yours, your content is yours, and your customer can always reach you at the same address regardless of what any app decides this quarter.

Social platforms fail businesses in mundane ways, not just dramatic ones: a Page flagged by an automated system days before a busy season, a WhatsApp number lost with a stolen phone and its chat history with it, an algorithm change that halves the reach you spent two years building. A website doesn't eliminate those risks from your social channels, but it means none of them can take you offline entirely. (For the full comparison, see [website vs. Facebook Page](/website-vs-facebook-page).)

## It signals legitimacy

Fairly or not, a real website still reads as more established and trustworthy to a new customer than a social page alone — particularly for higher-value purchases, corporate clients, or anyone doing due diligence before committing money. "Do they have a website?" is a background check customers run without thinking about it, and failing it quietly costs you the exact customers worth the most: the careful ones.

This cuts both ways, so be honest with yourself: a neglected, half-finished website can signal *less* credibility than none at all. If you build one, build one that covers [the elements that actually matter](/what-good-business-website-includes) — accurate contacts, real photos, clear services — or don't build it yet.

## It works while you're not online

A website doesn't require you to be actively posting to keep functioning. At 11pm, when you're asleep, it is still answering "where are you located?", "how much is this?", "are you open on Sunday?", and "how do I book?" — questions that otherwise wait for your reply or go to a competitor who answered first. For service businesses, a simple quote-request or booking form converts after-hours interest that messaging-only businesses simply lose.

## What it actually costs to get started

This is usually where the idea stalls. Many small business owners assume a "real" website is a large, out-of-reach expense. In practice, a simple landing page for a small Kenyan business typically starts in the tens of thousands of shillings — not hundreds of thousands — with more complex sites (e-commerce, booking systems, M-Pesa integration) costing more depending on what they need to do. See our [full breakdown of website costs in Kenya](/how-much-does-website-cost-kenya) for realistic ranges by project type, and get an actual quote before assuming it's out of budget. Most owners are surprised in the affordable direction.

## What to do this month

You don't need to do everything at once. A sensible sequence:

1. **This week:** claim your free [Google Business Profile](https://business.google.com/) so name searches show correct details immediately.
2. **This month:** register your domain name (cheap insurance, even before you build) and get one or two written quotes for a simple site.
3. **This quarter:** launch something small but complete — services, prices, contacts, WhatsApp link — and keep your social channels running as the conversation layer on top.

None of this means abandoning WhatsApp or Facebook. For many small businesses, social media is still where most day-to-day customer conversation happens, and that's fine. The strongest setup is a website as the credible, owned home base, with social media and WhatsApp feeding traffic into it — each channel doing the job it's actually good at.
