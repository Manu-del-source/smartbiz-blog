Eldoret has grown into one of Kenya's busiest regional trade and agricultural hubs, and the businesses competing for customers here — hotels, hardware stores, law firms, salons, agro suppliers — increasingly win or lose that competition online before a customer ever walks through the door. A website built without Eldoret's specific market in mind usually underperforms one that is. Here's what "built for Eldoret" actually means.

## Eldoret's market isn't Nairobi's market

Most web design advice online is written for Nairobi or for generic audiences, and a lot of it doesn't quite fit here:

- **Searches are landmark-based.** Customers search by neighborhood or landmark — "hardware shop near Huruma," "hotel near Eldoret town," "salon along Oloo Street" — more often than by brand name. Your site needs to mention your actual area in plain text, not just your business name.
- **Referrals still drive business — and your site confirms them.** Word of mouth sends the customer; Google confirms the decision. When someone hears about you and looks you up, your website's job is reassurance: a clear address, a working phone number, real photos of your actual premises. A referred customer who finds nothing (or finds a broken half-site) often quietly picks the competitor instead.
- **Flashy loses to fast.** Animation-heavy sites built to impress on office Wi-Fi frustrate visitors on phones and mobile data. Here, a clear address, working contacts, and a page that loads fast on a mid-range Android phone matter more than visual spectacle.

## Most of your visitors are on a phone, and data isn't free

The Communications Authority of Kenya's quarterly sector statistics reports (see [ca.go.ke](https://www.ca.go.ke/)) consistently show smartphones making up roughly two-thirds of connected mobile devices, with mobile broadband carrying the large majority of internet access. In practice: a website built and tested on a desktop browser, then assumed to "work fine" on mobile, is being judged by the wrong standard.

Every megabyte matters too. Mobile data costs your visitor money, so a heavy, unoptimized homepage doesn't just load slowly — it charges admission. Fast, lightweight, [mobile-first design](/mobile-friendly-websites-kenya) isn't a nice-to-have for an Eldoret business; it's the baseline. When a developer demos your site, ask to see it on a phone, on mobile data, before final payment. (More on that below.)

## Getting found by customers searching nearby

A website that exists but doesn't appear when someone in Eldoret searches for what you sell isn't doing its job. The highest-leverage steps, in order:

1. **Claim and complete your Google Business Profile** — accurate hours, your real location pinned correctly on the map, phone number, photos. This alone puts you on Maps, which is where "near me" searches get answered. Follow our [setup guide](/google-business-profile-setup-guide-kenya).
2. **Mention Eldoret and your neighborhood on the website itself** — in page titles, headings, and body text. "Plumber in Eldoret serving Huruma, Langas, and Kapsoya" matches real searches; "quality plumbing solutions" matches none.
3. **Collect genuine customer reviews** on your Google profile. In a Maps comparison between two similar businesses, reviews are often the deciding factor. See [how to get reviews](/get-online-reviews-kenya-business).
4. **Keep details consistent** — same business name, same phone number, same address on the site, the profile, and any directories.

The fuller version of this process is our [local SEO checklist](/how-to-get-found-on-google-kenya).

## What to look for in a web developer

Not every developer who can build *a* website can build one that works for a local Eldoret business. Check before you hire:

- **Relevant portfolio.** Have they built for businesses similar to yours, ideally in Kenya — and can you open the live sites yourself, not just screenshots? Click through on your phone. Slow or broken portfolio sites are disqualifying.
- **SEO literacy.** Can they explain, in plain terms, how they'll help you show up in local search — or is SEO an afterthought they'll "add later"? The right answer mentions page titles, location content, speed, and your Business Profile.
- **Realistic pricing and scope.** Compare against [typical website costs in Kenya](/how-much-does-website-cost-kenya). Vague quotes with no written scope are a warning sign; so are prices dramatically below market with no explanation of what's excluded.
- **A support plan after launch.** Websites need updates, security patches, and content changes. Ask who handles that, at what cost, and what happens if something breaks — before you need the answer. Our [maintenance guide](/website-maintenance-security-basics-kenya) explains what's actually involved.
- **Straightforward communication.** How they respond during the sales conversation is usually how they'll communicate during the project. Slow, vague, or jargon-heavy now means slow, vague, or jargon-heavy later.

## Questions worth asking before you sign

1. Will the site be built mobile-first, and can I test it on my own phone before final payment?
2. Who owns the domain name and hosting account once the project is done?
3. What happens if I want to add or change content myself later — can I, or does every edit cost extra?
4. Is there a maintenance or support plan, and exactly what does it cover and cost?
5. Will you help set up or optimize my Google Business Profile as part of the project?
6. Is this quote fixed for the stated scope, and how are extra requests priced?

Get the answers in writing. It takes one email and it prevents the most common regret local business owners report after a rushed website project: paying for something that looks fine but doesn't actually bring in customers.

## A realistic timeline

For a typical small-business site — a few pages, contact and WhatsApp integration, basic SEO setup — expect two to four weeks from content handover to launch, with most delays caused by content (photos, copy, decisions) rather than building. Anyone promising a custom site in 48 hours is selling a template with your name on it; anyone quoting three months for five pages without custom features should explain why. Agree the timeline and the content deadlines up front, in the same document as the price.
